## 1. The one structural idea

A normal founder spends human hours on outreach, demos, and content, and those hours are the funnel's bottleneck. Here the economics are inverted: agent output (research, monitoring, drafts, guides, reply queues, email answers) is effectively free and instant, and **human acts are the bottleneck** — there are exactly three of them: posting publicly, merging PRs, and approving money/scope.

Three design rules fall out of that:

1. **Over-invest in written assets.** Guides, the free tool, FAQ, reply drafts — the agent produces them at no marginal cost, and each one sells while nobody is present. The ladder at the bottom of every page is the sales team.
2. **Meter every play in Andy-minutes per public touch.** A play that needs 30 of your minutes per day dies in week two. Target: one 15-minute daily block (read the digest, paste approved replies, merge reviewed PRs) plus one or two scheduled bursts (Show HN morning).
3. **Match product to channel by buyer.** They're different people:

| Product | Buyer | Where they are | Motion |
|---|---|---|---|
| $99 pack | The IC dev stuck **tonight** | Forum / Reddit / HN / X threads, Google | Self-serve: thread → guide/tool → Stripe. No human needed. |
| $4,500 / $7,500 install | Eng lead or CTO who wants the loop without owning the wiring | LinkedIn, inbound email, referrals | Considered purchase: scoping email thread → payment → install. Expect longer than a week. |
| $1,500/mo retainer | Existing install customer | The install itself | Not an acquisition channel this week. |

## 5. Turning a useful reply into $99 or $4,500 without being slimy

**The governing rule: never withhold the answer to sell the answer.** Every free thing stays complete — the paste tool does the whole job, the guides contain the whole fix. What's for sale is packaging ($99: the skill, the prompt, the how-to, assembled), wiring ($4,500: done on your repo, proven with a dry-run PR), and accountability (the playbook, the guardrails, the "not a fit" answer). The moment a free asset feels like a teaser, the entire position collapses.

**The ladder, and where price is allowed to appear:**

```
public reply (complete fix, free)          ← no prices, no pitch
   → guide / paste.html (free, complete)   ← ladder appears at page bottom; site discloses everything
      → $99 pack (self-serve convenience)  ← price on the site only
         → $4,500 install (done-for-you + receipt PR)
            → $7,500 triggers / $1,500/mo retainer
```

**The two-touch rule.** First touch is only ever help. A second touch that mentions money is allowed only when the other person opens the door — "we have thirty of these a week," "can someone just set this up for us," "what do you charge." Then one plain paragraph (Appendix A2): what it costs, what it is, that the free path is genuinely fine for most, and where to email. No follow-up if they go quiet. Silence is an answer.

**Venue rules for links and prices:**
- Cursor forum: fix inline; guide link when it directly answers; no prices; profile carries the site.
- Reddit: follow each sub's rules; disclose "my site" on any link; no prices in-thread, ever.
- HN: fix inline; links fine with disclosure; prices only if directly asked, answered factually.
- X: fix in the reply text; links sparingly (first replies with links read as spam and travel worse).
- Nowhere: the Stripe checkout link. Public posts link pages, not checkouts.

**The inbox converts; here's its policy.**
- SLA: first response same day.
- Structure: diagnose first, in their terms, so they know they were read. Then the straight answer — including "you don't need the install for this" when true, with the free fix right there in the email. Honest disqualification is the highest-converting sentence this shop owns: the site promises "plain answers, including 'not a fit,'" and the inbox is where that promise is kept or broken.
- Price appears in email exactly when the conversation is about buying: scope, terms (payment first, 7 days, human merges, never main), and the two scoping questions (which repo, which ticket type). Skeletons in Appendix A6.
- Authorship split: informational replies may be agent-written and agent-sent — the site already discloses that `orders@` replies are AI-written, and each reply says so. Anything committing scope, price, or dates gets your sign-off before send.
- No drip. One thread, answer what's asked. A single "closing the loop" message inside an active scoping conversation is a live deal thread, not a sequence; anything beyond that is the funnel we promised not to run.

## 8. Risks, and staying AI-honest

**Ranked risks:**

1. **One bad reply, screenshotted forever.** The defense is procedural: every public draft passes the test *"would this read fine quoted in someone's 'AI spam is ruining forums' thread?"* — plus volume caps, no-price-on-first-touch, and your edit before anything posts.
2. **Cursor ships the fix and the wedge closes.** Good — for users, and survivable for us: the install's value is the loop (scoping, guardrails, tests, playbook, the merge discipline), not the bug. Duty that falls out of it: guides carry "last checked" dates and get **updated or retired** the week a fix ships. A stale guide quietly misleading people is an honesty failure, not just an SEO problem.
3. **Upstream relationship risk.** We earn near Cursor's rough edges. Tone stays neutral-to-helpful, never bashing; no ToS-violating workarounds; no implied affiliation; and in staff-active threads, silence is the default. Be the reply staff would be glad exists.
4. **Goodhart's law on the agent.** An agent told to acquire customers will optimize replies-posted unless told otherwise. The scoreboard is §7 — dollars and conversations, with process targets as floors, not goals to inflate.
5. **Thin market.** Four pains is a niche. When threads run dry, the answer is widening the *problem surface* (new adjacent failure modes as Cursor evolves), never widening the spam surface.
6. **Platform norms.** HN and Reddit punish anything that smells coordinated. Defenses: one identity, low volume, real usefulness, disclosure, and reading each venue's rules before posting cadence is set (§9 — not yet verified).
7. **Accidental autonomy.** The structural guarantee that outranks every promise: **the acq agent holds no posting credentials for any public platform.** Keep it that way. Public speech is physically yours.

**The AI-honesty rules, consolidated:**

- One identity: your real accounts, your hand, every public post.
- Standing disclosure lives on the site and in the email footer ("AI-written, and it says so"). Per-post disclosure whenever the post is about AI or the venue cares; never deny AI involvement anywhere.
- No number without a receipt. No testimonials until they're real and checkable. No logos, ever, without permission and a real engagement behind them.
- Free things stay complete. The disclosure is the demo — the about page already says it; the acquisition motion has to keep it true.
- When we don't know, we say so — in guides ("we won't invent a fix the thread doesn't support" is already live copy), in email, and in this memo (§9).
