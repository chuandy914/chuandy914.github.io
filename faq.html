The offer

What exactly is Crew Install?

A seven-day, done-for-you setup on one repo you own. We configure Cursor cloud agents so one ticket type you pick turns into branches, tests, and pull requests. It ends with a dry-run PR from your real backlog and a written playbook. A human on your team still merges everything — we never touch main.

What does “one ticket type” mean, exactly?

One narrow, recurring shape of work: reproducible bugs, dependency and cleanup chores, or small well-scoped features. Not “redesign onboarding.” The narrowness is what makes the loop reliable, and it’s why the install fits in seven days. If you’re not sure which type to pick, say so in the email and we’ll tell you what we’d pick.

What’s the difference between the $4,500 and $7,500 installs?

Same seven days, same guardrails. The $7,500 version also wires Slack or Linear as the trigger, so an issue or a message kicks the loop instead of someone starting the agent by hand.

What does the $1,500/mo retainer cover?

After an install, we keep the loop fed: up to eight tickets a month go through the crew — scoped, run, and shepherded to PR. Review and merge stay with your team. It requires a completed install; it isn’t a standalone product.

Why is the install $4,500 when the pack is $99?

They’re different products. The pack is instructions — you do the wiring. The install is the wiring done on your repo, proven with a real PR from your backlog, and handed over as a playbook your team keeps.

Is this a Cursor reseller or a Cursor subscription?

No. You bring your own Cursor plan; we configure it against your repo. The $99 Merge Crew pack isn’t a subscription to anything either — it’s a one-time download of the skill, the prompt, and the how-to.

The agents and the loop

Why can’t our Cursor cloud agent read GitHub issues?

Because the token the agent’s cloud VM actually receives often lacks issues scope — even when the GitHub App UI shows Issues read/write. It’s a known, documented gap, and the result is exactly what it looks like: tickets sit, or the agent works from the title and invents the rest. Two fixes work — paste the ticket into the prompt, or add one team GitHub token as a secret. The guide walks through both, step by step. Fixing ticket ingestion is the first thing the install does.

Who actually does the work?

AI agents do the implementation, and an AI operator runs the process — disclosed, not hidden. The judgment calls that matter (review, merge, revert) stay with your engineers. That split is the product.

What if the pull requests are bad?

Then you reject them — that’s the system working, not failing. The dry run on your real backlog exists so you see the quality inside the seven days, not after them. A bad PR costs your engineer a review, not an incident.

What happens after day 7?

You keep everything. The configuration lives in your repo, and the playbook explains how to run the next ticket, tune the guardrails, and revoke our access. Run it yourselves, or put the retainer on it. There’s no lock-in mechanism — on purpose.

Which ticket type should we pick?

One type, small and well-scoped: reproducible bugs, chores, dependency and cleanup work, or small features. Not “redesign the product.” If you’re unsure, say so in the email and we’ll tell you what we’d pick.

Does this work with GitLab, Bitbucket, or Jira?

The install is built around Cursor cloud agents and GitHub — repo, branches, pull requests. Slack or Linear can be the trigger on the $7,500 version. If your stack lives elsewhere, email us anyway: if we can’t wire it, we’ll say so instead of improvising on your infrastructure.

Access and safety

Will you ever merge into our repo?

No. The install has no auto-merge path and we never push to main. A human on your team merges every PR — or it doesn’t merge.

What access do you need?

One repo, scoped: enough for agents to push branches and open pull requests. Branch protection on main stays exactly as you have it. The playbook covers rotating and revoking everything we set up.

Do we have to hand you a GitHub token?

You never send us a token — not by email, not in a ticket, not anywhere. If your setup needs one so agents can read issues (see the guide), you create it, scope it read-only to the one repo, and put it in your own secret store. We tell you exactly which scopes; the credential stays in your hands.

What happens to our code?

The work happens inside your repo, on your Cursor plan, under your access controls. We don’t take a copy of your codebase, and nothing of yours is used to build anything of ours. What Cursor’s agents do with your code is governed by your agreement with Cursor — read it, and ask us if you want the relevant parts pointed out.
